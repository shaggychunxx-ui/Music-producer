---PAGE writing-chord-progressions ---
Diatonic major is the default launch pad (Jake's estimate: most public music).
C major notes: C D E F G A B. Formula W-W-H-W-W-W-H.
Diatonic triads: I ii iii IV V vi vii°  (C Dm Em F G Am Bdim).
Beginners ignore vii° at first.

Three rules for a first loop:
1. Write four measures.
2. Start on I (tonic).
3. End the loop on IV or V so it wants to return to I.

IV→I = plagal (soft landing). V→I = authentic (decisive).
Repeating a chord (e.g. V for two bars) is allowed.
Fill the middle with any of I–vi. Guessing still works if the three rules hold.

Most popular modern loop: I–V–vi–IV
  G: G D Em C
  C: C G Am F
Examples Jake lists: I'm Yours, Paparazzi chorus, Someone Like You chorus, What's My Age Again chorus.

95% of writing is methodical; ~5% is effortless inspiration. Brute-force a loop when stuck.
Practice: a new key every day. Electronic: program the same loops as strings/synths.

Next expansions (same series): 7ths, inversions, secondary dominants, borrowed chords, key changes.

Watch: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=M8eItITv8QA

---PAGE writing-chord-progressions-series ---
Full writer ladder:
1. Pick major or minor; write diatonic triads + roman numerals.
2. 4- or 8-chord loop starting on tonic.
3. Upgrade to diatonic sevenths (I/IV = maj7, V = 7, ii/iii/vi = m7).
4. Inversions so the bass is not glued to roots.
5. Sus2/sus4 to delay color or stretch one chord.
6. Secondary dominants (leave the key, land back in it).
7. Passing dim7 between chords a whole step apart.
8. Borrow from parallel modes (easiest: parallel minor).
9. Modulate.

Minor starter palette: i, iv, bVI, bVII. Any mix works.
Then "break" minor with V (needs raised 7 / harmonic minor). That V–i is the Andalusian last move when the first three chords are i–bVII–bVI.

Dominant vocabulary:
- THE dominant = V (major triad on degree 5), in any key.
- A dominant chord = dominant-7th quality (1 3 5 b7). Not the same phrase.

Inversions: C → G/B → Am → F gives a bass half-step C–B–A instead of C–G–A–F.
Sus: almost every diatonic triad can become sus2 or sus4. Exceptions Jake flags: iii usually only sus2; IV usually sus4 not sus2.

Secondary dominant example in C: C – E7 – Am – G  (E7 = V7/vi). Mini-modulation, then back.

Passing dim7: C – F – F#dim7 – G plugs the whole-step gap IV–V.

Borrowed from parallel minor in C:
  C – Am – F – Fm        (iv)
  C – F – Ab – G         (bVI)
  C – Bb – F – G         (bVII)

Do not switch scales per chord on a diatonic loop. One parent scale covers all seven chords.

---PAGE writing-in-minor ---
Minor roman numerals (C minor): i ii° bIII iv v bVI bVII.
Beginner loops: i, iv, bVI, bVII.
Adding V (major) is extremely common even though it is not natural-minor. Use harmonic minor (raised 7) over that V.

---PAGE andalusian-cadence ---
Pattern: i – bVII – bVI – V
Em: Em D C B
Dm: Dm C Bb A
Am: Am G F E

First three chords are Aeolian. Final V has the leading tone (D# in Em) → harmonic minor over V only.

Style transformations of the SAME numerals:
- Latin/flamenco: sus4 ornaments, bVI add#11, from the home minor scale.
- Metal: power chords + palm mute + syncopation; minor over first three, harmonic minor over V; do not only loop — write a longer section.
- Psychedelia: pedal/drone the tonic under i–bVII–bVI so the bass does not walk; pads/synths.

You will not invent a new 4-chord recipe. Originality is rhythm, inversion, voicing, arpeggio, and arrangement.

Examples: Runaway (Del Shannon), Stray Cat Strut, Sultans of Swing (hidden), Walk Don't Run.
Watch: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qbeRVJMT5CY

---PAGE seven-ways-to-use-modes ---
Article details the first three of seven Codex methods.

1) Stay inside one mode.
   A Dorian triads: Am Bm C D Em F#° G
   Notes alone are not enough — promote the tonic (A / Am) as home. Easy: two bars of Am, then IV (D), then bIII (Cmaj7). Melody stays in A Dorian.

2) Write in the mode, then break it on purpose.
   A Dorian wants G. G# (leading tone) + E major (V) is a desirable break. Melody over E should use G# not G. This is also the door to melodic minor (raise Dorian's 7).
   Example: Am Am D E.

3) Relative modulation.
   Same seven notes, new tonic. Verse B minor / chorus D major = Comfortably Numb.
   Demo: E Phrygian → A Aeolian (same notes as C major / A minor). Ear prefers landing on the more stable Aeolian.

Remaining methods (watch the video; Codex ch.21): parallel-mode color, modal interchange, modal melody over mixed harmony, and mode-as-scale-choice over a vamp/chord. Expand later to harmonic/melodic minor modes.

Watch: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sC2qXLnVU3A

---PAGE understanding-the-modes ---
Teach PARALLEL modes first (same root, different flavors). Relative "start on degree 2" is technically true but a bad first frame — the ear still hears the parent tonic.

To actually be in a mode: reinforce a tonal center that is not Ionian's I. Drown the ear in that pitch + its tonic triad.
  Notes of C, home = C → C Ionian
  Notes of C, home = Dm → D Dorian
  Notes of C, home = G → G Mixolydian

Guitar shortcut: one scale shape, change which note is home (minor pentatonic box can be reused this way).

Tonic quality groups:
  Major tonic: Ionian (default bright), Lydian (dreamier), Mixolydian (rougher/rock)
  Minor tonic: Aeolian (default dark), Dorian (wistful / nat6), Phrygian (evil / b2)
  Locrian: diminished tonic — unstable, almost never a home.

Dorian: raise Aeolian 6. Signature move i–IV (Am–D). Solo: promote nat6.
Phrygian: b2 is the Jaws half-step. Pedal the tonic; use bII or bvii (both contain b2). Full-song Phrygian is rare; dip in, then leave. Phrygian Dominant (harmonic-minor 5th) has a major 3rd — different scale.
Lydian: raise 4. Identity notes 1–3–#4–5. Stay on I; dip to II or vii. Avoid camping on V or the ear resets to that major key. Fragile.
Mixolydian: lower 7. Signature I–bVII–IV (G–F–C, AC/DC). Also I–v. Mixolydian pentatonic for leads.
Aeolian: "in minor" usually also uses harmonic minor to get V.
Locrian: leftover. Diminished tonic will not sit still. Know why it fails; use as a color over dim chords.

---PAGE modal-mixture-major-keys ---
Writing in the KEY of A major ≠ using only the A major SCALE. Key = A major triad is home. Outside notes (b7, b6, #4) are normal.

Borrowed chords do not "come from" one place. bVII (G in A) lives in Mixolydian, Dorian, and Aeolian. You prove an origin by the scale you play on top.

Poke-one-note model:
  Lower 7 of major → Mixolydian / neighbor key (A major vs D major share all but G#/G).
  Raise 4 of major → Lydian / other neighbor (A vs E).
  Lower 3+6+7 → parallel minor (bigger jump, quarter-turn on the circle).

Avoid conflict: do not play G# over a G triad, or G over E (two thirds = blues color, clouds rich harmony).

Extensions disambiguate origin: Amaj7 cannot be Mixolydian; Gmaj7 cannot be A Aeolian (that would be G7).

Neighbor-key thinking is optional. Prefer: "I'm in A, with a flexible 7th (or 4th)."

When a chord needs only one foreign note, do not automatically jump to full parallel minor. iv (Dm in A) only needs F; A harmonic major (lower 6 only) or Mixolydian b6 can fit and keep the major 3rd. Weezer Buddy Holly lick keeps C# over Dm. Layla closing Bb7 in C: Mixolydian b6 preserves the major 3rd.

---PAGE voice-leading ---
Think notes inside chords, not only "chord X then Y."
Melodic momentum: stepwise motion creates expectation in that direction.
Neighbor tones (half or whole step off a chord tone) make the arrival feel wanted.
Expendable fifth: drop 5 to free a voice or to voice a 7th chord in 3 parts.

Four motions:
  Parallel — same direction, same interval class
  Similar — same direction, different intervals
  Contrary — opposite directions (V7 tritone resolving is a classic)
  Oblique — one voice moves, one stays

Demo IV–V–I in C as 3 voices:
  Bass: F G C
  Mid:  A B C
  Top:  C D E
Each line is smooth; 5ths optional.

---PAGE 7-simple-rhythm-exercises ---
No metronome first. Sync body + voice + instrument, then add click.

1. Six even notes. Foot every 3 ("1 2 3") = 6/8 feel. Foot every 2 ("1 2") = 3/4 feel.
2. Pattern grouped in 3s; switch foot grouping. Clap on 1s, then clap every 2 against a 3-count.
3. Pattern grouped in 2s; switch foot grouping. Clap on 1s, then clap every 3 against a 2-count.
4. Twelve even notes. Foot every 3 vs every 4 (12/8 vs 3/4 / 4-feel).
5. Group-of-3 riff felt in 3-pulse vs 4-pulse.
6. Group-of-4 riff felt in 3-pulse vs 4-pulse. This is 12/8's dual world — gateway to metric modulation (same pattern, new pulse = new tempo).
7. Add rests (e.g. four-note group with 4 silent). "The Mirror" (Dream Theater) opening is this idea.

Polymeter ≈ two meters at one tempo (foot vs hands in these drills).
Polyrhythm ≈ keep only the first attack of each polymetric voice.

---PAGE stealing-musical-ideas ---
Steal a RHYTHM, not a melody. Double tresillo = 3+3+3+3+4. Write original pitches on that grid. Arrangement can change after the mix (Jake dropped the riff over a Picardy third).

---PAGE secondary-dominants ---
Non-diatonic chords that resolve to a diatonic target. Mini-modulations. V7/vi is especially common in modern music. Work best in stable major/minor; harder in fragile modes.
Watch: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=py4HaueW50Q

---PAGE inversions-slash-chords ---
Root-position only = bass leaps with the roots. Inversions free a stepwise bass. First inversion = 6/3 = C/E. Second = 6/4 = C/G. Slash chord = Chord/BassNote.
Watch: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LFN-eKved_8

---PAGE mario-cadence ---
I – bVI – bVII – I. Modal mixture. Super Mario Bros, I Am the Walrus, Kiss From A Rose.

---PAGE power-chords ---
Root+5 (no 3). Ambiguous major/minor — can sit under either quality. Building block of rock/metal riffs.

---PAGE lofi-production ---
Lo-fi "slop" can be designed (unconventional meters, e.g. 33/32) rather than only vinyl noise. Harmony can hover between relative major/minor.
